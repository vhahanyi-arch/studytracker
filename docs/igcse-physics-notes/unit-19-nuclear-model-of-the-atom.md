# Unit 19 — The nuclear model of the atom

Source: Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus, sections 5.1.1 and 5.1.2, Core and Supplement. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Concept summary

### The atom — 5.1.1

An atom has a tiny, positively charged nucleus at its centre, with negatively charged electrons in orbit around it. Most of an atom is empty space. A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons, so the charges balance.

**Ions:** an atom that loses one or more electrons becomes a positive ion; an atom that gains electrons becomes a negative ion. The nucleus does not change.

### Evidence for the nucleus: alpha scattering — 5.1.1 (Supplement)

Alpha particles (small, positive, fast) were fired at a very thin sheet of gold foil in a vacuum, and a detector recorded where they went.

| Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Almost all alpha particles went straight through, undeflected | The atom is mostly empty space; the nucleus is very small |
| A few were deflected through small angles | The nucleus is positively charged: it repels the positive alpha particles that pass close to it |
| A very few (about 1 in 8000) bounced back through more than 90° | The nucleus contains most of the mass of the atom, concentrated in a tiny volume, so a head-on alpha particle is turned back |

This replaced the earlier "plum pudding" model, in which positive charge was spread through the whole atom.

### The nucleus — 5.1.2

The nucleus is made of protons and neutrons, together called nucleons.

| Particle | Relative charge | Relative mass | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | +1 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Neutron | 0 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Electron | −1 | about 1/1840 (negligible) | Orbiting the nucleus |

- **Proton number (atomic number), Z:** the number of protons in the nucleus. It decides which element the atom is.
- **Nucleon number (mass number), A:** the total number of protons and neutrons.
- **Number of neutrons, N = A − Z.**

**Nuclide notation:** the nucleon number is written at the top left of the element's symbol and the proton number at the bottom left. For example `^12_6 C` is carbon with 6 protons and 12 − 6 = 6 neutrons; `^235_92 U` is uranium with 92 protons and 143 neutrons.

**Isotopes** are atoms of the same element (same proton number) with different numbers of neutrons (different nucleon numbers). An element may have several isotopes: `^12_6 C`, `^13_6 C` and `^14_6 C` are all carbon. Isotopes have the same chemical properties, because they have the same number of electrons, but different masses, and some are radioactive.

**Supplement:** the relative charge of a nucleus equals its proton number Z (each proton is +1, neutrons are 0). The relative mass of a nucleus equals its nucleon number A (each nucleon has a relative mass of 1).

### Fission and fusion — 5.1.2 (Supplement)

**Nuclear fission** is the splitting of a large, unstable nucleus into two smaller nuclei (daughter nuclei), usually after it absorbs a neutron. Two or three neutrons are also released, along with a large amount of energy. The released neutrons can cause further fissions: a chain reaction, controlled in a nuclear power station.

`^1_0 n + ^235_92 U → ^141_56 Ba + ^92_36 Kr + 3 ^1_0 n`

**Nuclear fusion** is the joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus, releasing a large amount of energy. It happens in stars, where hydrogen nuclei fuse to make helium; it needs a very high temperature and pressure so the positive nuclei move fast enough to overcome their repulsion.

`^2_1 H + ^3_1 H → ^4_2 He + ^1_0 n`

In both processes the total mass of the products is slightly less than the total mass before. This "lost" mass has been converted into energy, released mainly as kinetic energy of the products. In every nuclear equation, the nucleon numbers balance and the proton numbers balance.

## Using the formulas in different situations

### Situation: count the particles

**Example — sodium.** `^23_11 Na` has 11 protons, 23 − 11 = 12 neutrons, and in a neutral atom 11 electrons.

**Example — an ion.** A `^27_13 Al` ion with a charge of +3 has 13 protons, 14 neutrons and 13 − 3 = 10 electrons.

**Example — a negative ion.** A `^35_17 Cl` ion with charge −1 has 17 protons, 18 neutrons and 17 + 1 = 18 electrons.

### Situation: identify isotopes

**Example.** Which two of these are isotopes of the same element: `^14_6 X`, `^14_7 Y`, `^12_6 Z`? X and Z: both have proton number 6. Y has the same nucleon number as X but a different proton number, so it is a different element.

### Situation: write a nuclide from a description

**Example.** A nucleus has 26 protons and 30 neutrons and is iron, Fe. A = 26 + 30 = 56, so it is `^56_26 Fe`.

### Situation: complete a fission equation (Supplement)

**Example — find the missing nucleus.** `^1_0 n + ^235_92 U → ^144_55 Cs + ? + 2 ^1_0 n`

- Nucleon numbers: 1 + 235 = 236 = 144 + A + 2, so A = 90.
- Proton numbers: 0 + 92 = 92 = 55 + Z + 0, so Z = 37 (rubidium).
- Missing nucleus: `^90_37 Rb`.

### Situation: complete a fusion equation (Supplement)

**Example.** `^2_1 H + ^2_1 H → ? + ^1_0 n`

- A: 2 + 2 = 4 = A + 1, so A = 3. Z: 1 + 1 = 2 = Z + 0, so Z = 2.
- Missing nucleus: `^3_2 He` (helium-3).

### Situation: compare charge and mass (Supplement)

**Example.** Compare `^238_92 U` and `^4_2 He`. Relative charges: +92 and +2. Relative masses: 238 and 4. The uranium nucleus has 46 times the charge and about 60 times the mass.

## Exam checks

- Nucleon number on top, proton number below.
- Neutrons = nucleon number − proton number.
- Isotopes: same protons, different neutrons. Same element, same chemistry.
- Ions change the number of electrons, never the nucleus.
- Alpha scattering: "most went straight through" means mostly empty space; "a few bounced back" means a small, dense, positive nucleus.
- In fission and fusion equations, balance both the top numbers and the bottom numbers; mass decreases slightly and energy is released.
