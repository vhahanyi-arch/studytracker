# Unit 7 — Pressure

Source: Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus, section 1.8, Core and Supplement. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Concept summary

### Pressure on a surface — 1.8

Pressure is the force per unit area, acting at right angles (perpendicular) to a surface. Unit: pascal (Pa); 1 Pa = 1 N/m².

For the same force, a smaller area gives a larger pressure; a larger area gives a smaller pressure.

- Large pressure wanted (small area): a sharp knife, a needle, a drawing pin's point, football studs, the narrow heel of a shoe that dents a floor.
- Small pressure wanted (large area): snowshoes and skis, wide tyres on tractors, the wide head of a drawing pin against your thumb, the foundations of a building, a camel's wide feet on sand.

### Pressure in liquids — 1.8

Pressure beneath the surface of a liquid:

- increases with depth, because there is a greater weight of liquid above;
- increases with the density of the liquid;
- acts equally in all directions at a given depth;
- does not depend on the shape or width of the container.

This is why a dam is built thicker at the bottom, why water spurts furthest from the lowest hole in a can, and why divers' ears hurt as they go deeper.

**Supplement:** the change in pressure between two depths is the density × g × the change in depth. At the surface the pressure is atmospheric pressure (about 100 000 Pa), so the total pressure at a depth is atmospheric pressure plus the liquid's pressure.

## Key formulas

| Formula | Symbols and units | When to use it | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| p = F ÷ A | p: pressure, Pa (N/m²); F: force perpendicular to the surface, N; A: area, m² | Solids pressing on surfaces; force on an area from a known pressure: F = pA. | Core |
| Δp = ρgΔh | Δp: change in pressure, Pa; ρ: density of the liquid, kg/m³; g: 9.8 N/kg; Δh: change in depth, m | Pressure caused by a column of liquid. | Supplement |
| total pressure at depth = atmospheric pressure + ρgh | Pa | When the question asks for the total (absolute) pressure, not just the extra pressure from the liquid. | Supplement |

Area conversions: 1 m² = 10 000 cm² (100 × 100) and 1 cm² = 0.0001 m². This is the most common mistake in pressure questions.

## Using the formulas in different situations

### Situation: an object standing on a surface

**Example — block in different orientations.** A block of weight 60 N measures 0.10 m × 0.20 m × 0.30 m. What are the largest and smallest pressures it can exert on the floor?

- Smallest area: 0.10 × 0.20 = 0.020 m². Largest pressure = 60 ÷ 0.020 = 3000 Pa.
- Largest area: 0.20 × 0.30 = 0.060 m². Smallest pressure = 60 ÷ 0.060 = 1000 Pa.

**Example — area in cm².** A 700 N person stands on both feet with a total contact area of 350 cm².

- A = 350 cm² = 350 ÷ 10 000 = 0.035 m².
- p = 700 ÷ 0.035 = 20 000 Pa.
- On one foot the area halves and the pressure doubles to 40 000 Pa.

**Example — weight from mass.** A 1200 kg car rests on four tyres, each with 0.015 m² in contact with the road.

- F = W = 1200 × 9.8 = 11 760 N.
- A = 4 × 0.015 = 0.060 m².
- p = 11 760 ÷ 0.060 = 196 000 Pa.

### Situation: find the force or the area

**Example — force from pressure.** Air pressure of 100 000 Pa acts on a window of area 1.5 m². F = pA = 100 000 × 1.5 = 150 000 N. (It does not break because the same pressure pushes from the other side.)

**Example — area needed.** A building of weight 2.0 × 10⁷ N stands on ground that can take at most 400 000 Pa. A = F ÷ p = 2.0 × 10⁷ ÷ 400 000 = 50 m² of foundations.

### Situation: pressure in a liquid (Supplement)

**Example — swimming pool.** What is the pressure due to the water at the bottom of a 2.5 m deep pool? (Water density 1000 kg/m³.)

- Δp = 1000 × 9.8 × 2.5 = 24 500 Pa.
- Total pressure there = 100 000 + 24 500 = 124 500 Pa.

**Example — between two depths.** A diver descends from 5.0 m to 15 m in sea water (density 1030 kg/m³). Δp = 1030 × 9.8 × 10 = 100 940 Pa: roughly one extra atmosphere for every 10 m.

**Example — find the depth.** A sensor in a lake reads an extra pressure of 49 000 Pa. Δh = Δp ÷ ρg = 49 000 ÷ (1000 × 9.8) = 5.0 m.

**Example — different liquid.** At the same 0.20 m depth, mercury (13 600 kg/m³) gives 13 600 × 9.8 × 0.20 = 26 656 Pa, but water gives only 1000 × 9.8 × 0.20 = 1960 Pa. Greater density, greater pressure.

### Situation: force on a submerged surface (Supplement)

**Example — submarine hatch.** A hatch of area 0.50 m² is 40 m below the surface of sea water (1030 kg/m³).

- Pressure from the water = 1030 × 9.8 × 40 = 403 760 Pa.
- Force = pA = 403 760 × 0.50 = 201 880 N, about 2.0 × 10⁵ N.

## Exam checks

- Area must be in m² for an answer in Pa. Divide cm² by 10 000.
- The force in p = F ÷ A is the force perpendicular to the surface; for an object resting on the ground it is its weight, mg.
- Liquid pressure depends on depth and density only, not on the container's shape or the total volume.
- Δh is the vertical depth below the surface, not the distance along a slope.
- Use "increases with depth because the weight of liquid above increases" for describe-and-explain questions.
