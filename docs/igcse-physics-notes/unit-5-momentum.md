# Unit 5 — Momentum

Source: Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus, section 1.6, Supplement. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Concept summary

### Momentum — 1.6 (Supplement)

Momentum is mass × velocity. It is a vector, so it has a direction; choose one direction as positive and give velocities in the other direction a minus sign. Unit: kg m/s. A lorry and a bicycle moving at the same velocity have very different momenta, because the lorry's mass is far greater.

### Impulse — 1.6 (Supplement)

Impulse is force × the time for which the force acts. An impulse changes an object's momentum by exactly that amount: impulse = FΔt = Δ(mv). Unit: N s, which is the same as kg m/s.

The same change in momentum can come from a large force for a short time or a small force for a long time. Safety features use this: crumple zones, airbags, seat belts, crash mats, cycle helmets and bending your knees when you land all increase the time over which the momentum changes, so the force is smaller.

### Resultant force and momentum — 1.6 (Supplement)

Resultant force is the change in momentum per unit time: F = Δp ÷ Δt. This is another form of F = ma, because Δp ÷ Δt = m(Δv ÷ Δt) = ma when the mass is constant.

### Conservation of momentum — 1.6 (Supplement)

When objects collide or push apart (explode), the total momentum before equals the total momentum after, provided no external resultant force acts on them. Kinetic energy is usually not conserved in a collision (some becomes internal energy and sound), but momentum always is.

- Collision where the objects stick together: they move off with one shared velocity.
- Collision where they bounce: each has its own velocity afterwards; one may reverse.
- Explosion or recoil from rest: total momentum before is zero, so afterwards the momenta are equal in size and opposite in direction. A gun recoils when it fires a bullet; a skater pushing off another skater moves backward.

## Key formulas

| Formula | Symbols and units | When to use it | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| p = mv | p: momentum, kg m/s; m: mass, kg; v: velocity, m/s | Momentum of one object. Keep the sign of v. | Supplement |
| impulse = FΔt = Δ(mv) | F: force, N; Δt: time the force acts, s; Δ(mv) = mv − mu, kg m/s or N s | A force acting for a time, such as a kick, a crash or a catch. | Supplement |
| F = Δp ÷ Δt | F: resultant force, N; Δp: change in momentum, kg m/s; Δt: time taken, s | Average force during an impact, or force from a momentum change. | Supplement |
| total momentum before = total momentum after | m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ (u before, v after) | Collisions and explosions with no external resultant force. | Supplement |

Δp is always final momentum minus initial momentum. If an object bounces back, its final velocity has the opposite sign, so the change is bigger than either momentum alone.

## Using the formulas in different situations

### Situation: momentum of one object

**Example — running.** A 60 kg student runs at 5.0 m/s. p = 60 × 5.0 = 300 kg m/s in the direction of running.

**Example — find the speed.** A 0.20 kg puck has momentum 1.2 kg m/s. v = p ÷ m = 1.2 ÷ 0.20 = 6.0 m/s.

### Situation: impulse and average force

**Example — kicking a ball.** A 0.45 kg football at rest is kicked to 20 m/s. The boot is in contact for 0.015 s.

- Δp = 0.45 × 20 − 0 = 9.0 kg m/s (the impulse is 9.0 N s).
- F = Δp ÷ Δt = 9.0 ÷ 0.015 = 600 N.

**Example — a ball that bounces.** A 0.10 kg ball hits a wall at 8.0 m/s and rebounds at 6.0 m/s. Contact time 0.020 s. Take towards the wall as positive.

- Before: 0.10 × 8.0 = +0.80 kg m/s. After: 0.10 × (−6.0) = −0.60 kg m/s.
- Δp = −0.60 − 0.80 = −1.40 kg m/s (size 1.4 kg m/s, away from the wall).
- F = 1.40 ÷ 0.020 = 70 N, away from the wall.

### Situation: safety features (longer stopping time)

**Example — airbag.** A 70 kg driver moving at 15 m/s is stopped in a crash.

- Δp = 70 × 15 = 1050 kg m/s whatever stops them.
- Stopped by a dashboard in 0.010 s: F = 1050 ÷ 0.010 = 105 000 N.
- Stopped by an airbag in 0.15 s: F = 1050 ÷ 0.15 = 7000 N, fifteen times smaller.

### Situation: collision, objects stick together

**Example — trolleys.** A 2.0 kg trolley at 3.0 m/s hits a stationary 1.0 kg trolley and they join.

- Before: 2.0 × 3.0 + 1.0 × 0 = 6.0 kg m/s.
- After: (2.0 + 1.0) × v = 6.0, so v = 2.0 m/s in the original direction.

### Situation: head-on collision, objects moving towards each other

**Example — opposite directions.** A 3.0 kg ball moving right at 4.0 m/s hits a 1.0 kg ball moving left at 2.0 m/s. They stick together. Right is positive.

- Before: 3.0 × 4.0 + 1.0 × (−2.0) = 12 − 2 = 10 kg m/s.
- After: 4.0 × v = 10, so v = 2.5 m/s to the right.

### Situation: collision, objects bounce apart

**Example — find one velocity.** A 0.50 kg ball at 6.0 m/s hits a stationary 1.5 kg ball. Afterwards the 1.5 kg ball moves forward at 2.5 m/s.

- Before: 0.50 × 6.0 = 3.0 kg m/s.
- After: 0.50 × v + 1.5 × 2.5 = 3.0, so 0.50v = 3.0 − 3.75 = −0.75, and v = −1.5 m/s.
- The minus sign means the 0.50 kg ball bounces back at 1.5 m/s.

### Situation: explosion or recoil from rest

**Example — rifle.** A 4.0 kg rifle fires a 0.010 kg bullet at 400 m/s.

- Total momentum before = 0.
- After: 0.010 × 400 + 4.0 × v = 0, so 4.0v = −4.0, and v = −1.0 m/s.
- The rifle recoils at 1.0 m/s, opposite to the bullet.

**Example — skaters.** Two skaters of 50 kg and 75 kg stand together and push apart. The 50 kg skater moves off at 3.0 m/s. The 75 kg skater moves the other way at (50 × 3.0) ÷ 75 = 2.0 m/s.

### Situation: force from a momentum change per second

**Example — water jet.** A hose sends 2.0 kg of water each second at 10 m/s onto a wall, where it stops. Δp per second = 2.0 × 10 = 20 kg m/s each second, so the force on the wall is 20 N.

## Exam checks

- Choose a positive direction and keep signs on every velocity.
- Momentum is conserved in every collision and explosion with no external force; kinetic energy usually is not.
- In a stick-together collision, use the combined mass after.
- An explosion from rest has zero total momentum: equal and opposite momenta.
- Explain safety features in words: longer time for the same change in momentum gives a smaller force (F = Δp ÷ Δt).
- N s and kg m/s are the same unit.
