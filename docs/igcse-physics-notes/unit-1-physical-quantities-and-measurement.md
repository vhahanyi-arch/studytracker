# Unit 1 — Physical quantities and measurement

Source: Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus, section 1.1, Core and Supplement. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Concept summary

### Measuring length, volume and time — 1.1

Every measurement is a number and a unit, such as 25.0 cm. A ruler measures length; read it with your eye level with the mark to avoid parallax error, and measure from the zero mark (or subtract the starting reading if the zero is worn). A measuring cylinder measures the volume of a liquid; read the bottom of the meniscus at eye level, with the cylinder on a flat surface. 1 cm³ = 1 ml, and 1000 cm³ = 1 litre.

Clocks and stopwatches measure time intervals. A hand-operated stopwatch includes your reaction time (about 0.2 to 0.3 s) at both the start and the stop, so it is unreliable for short intervals. Digital timers started and stopped by light gates remove reaction time and suit short, fast events.

### Measuring multiples to reduce uncertainty — 1.1

Small lengths and short times are hard to measure directly, so measure many together and divide. Measure the thickness of 100 sheets of paper and divide by 100. Time 20 oscillations of a pendulum and divide by 20 to get the period. The reading uncertainty (and reaction time) is spread over all the repeats, so the average for one is far more precise. Repeat the whole measurement and take the mean to reduce the effect of random errors.

One oscillation of a pendulum is a complete swing: from one side, to the other, and back to the start. Start timing as the bob passes the centre (it moves fastest there, so the moment is easy to judge), and count "zero" as you start the watch.

### Scalars and vectors — 1.1 (Supplement)

A scalar has magnitude (size) only. A vector has magnitude and direction.

- Scalars: distance, speed, time, mass, energy, temperature.
- Vectors: force, weight, velocity, acceleration, momentum, electric field strength, gravitational field strength.

Speed and velocity show the difference: 20 m/s is a speed; 20 m/s due north is a velocity. Two cars moving at 20 m/s in opposite directions have the same speed but different velocities.

### Adding two vectors at right angles — 1.1 (Supplement)

The resultant is the single vector that has the same effect as the two together. For two vectors at 90° to each other (forces or velocities only in this syllabus), draw them tip-to-tail: the resultant runs from the tail of the first to the tip of the second, closing a right-angled triangle.

By calculation, Pythagoras gives the size and tan gives the angle. By scale drawing, choose a scale (for example 1 cm = 1 N), draw the two vectors at right angles, draw the resultant, measure its length and convert back, and measure the angle with a protractor. Always state the direction of the resultant as well as its size.

## Key formulas

| Formula | Symbols and units | When to use it | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| value of one = total measurement ÷ number measured | For example: period T = total time ÷ number of oscillations | Small distances and short times measured as multiples. | Core |
| mean = sum of readings ÷ number of readings | Same unit as the readings | Repeated readings of the same quantity. Leave out an obvious anomaly. | Core |
| resultant R = √(X² + Y²) | X, Y: the two perpendicular vectors (N for forces, m/s for velocities); R: same unit | Two vectors at exactly 90°. | Supplement |
| tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent = Y ÷ X | θ: angle of the resultant measured from X, in degrees | Gives the direction of the resultant. | Supplement |

Useful conversions: 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm; 1 km = 1000 m; 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³; 1 cm³ = 1 ml; 1 kg = 1000 g; 1 minute = 60 s; 1 hour = 3600 s.

## Using the formulas in different situations

### Situation: a stack or a coil of identical things

**Example — thickness of one sheet.** A stack of 250 sheets of paper is 2.5 cm thick.

- Thickness of one sheet = 2.5 cm ÷ 250 = 0.010 cm = 0.10 mm.
- The same method gives the diameter of a thin wire: wind 30 tight turns around a pencil, measure the width of the coil, divide by 30.

### Situation: timing a pendulum

**Example — period.** A student times 20 complete oscillations and gets 31.2 s, 30.8 s and 31.0 s in three trials.

- Mean time for 20 oscillations = (31.2 + 30.8 + 31.0) ÷ 3 = 31.0 s.
- Period T = 31.0 s ÷ 20 = 1.55 s.
- Why not time one swing? A reaction time of about 0.2 s would be a large fraction of 1.55 s; over 31 s it hardly matters.

### Situation: volume by measuring cylinder

**Example — volume of liquid poured out.** A measuring cylinder reads 74 cm³ before and 46 cm³ after some liquid is poured out.

- Volume poured = 74 − 46 = 28 cm³.

### Situation: two forces at right angles (Supplement)

**Example — resultant force.** A boat is pulled by a 30 N force east and a 40 N force north.

- R = √(30² + 40²) = √(900 + 1600) = √2500 = 50 N.
- tan θ = 40 ÷ 30 = 1.33, so θ = 53° north of east.
- Answer: 50 N at 53° north of east (a bearing of 037°).

### Situation: two velocities at right angles (Supplement)

**Example — swimmer crossing a river.** A swimmer swims at 1.2 m/s straight across a river that flows at 0.5 m/s.

- Resultant velocity = √(1.2² + 0.5²) = √(1.44 + 0.25) = √1.69 = 1.3 m/s.
- tan θ = 0.5 ÷ 1.2 = 0.417, so θ = 23° downstream from the straight-across direction.

### Situation: scale drawing (Supplement)

**Example — drawing the resultant.** Forces of 6.0 N and 8.0 N act at right angles. Using 1 cm = 1 N, draw 6.0 cm and 8.0 cm lines at 90°, tip-to-tail. The closing side measures 10.0 cm, so the resultant is 10 N. Measure the angle at the start of the 6.0 N line with a protractor: about 53°.

## Exam checks

- Give a unit with every measured or calculated value.
- When measuring multiples, say what you measured, how many, and that you divided by that number.
- Name the error you are avoiding: parallax (eye level), zero error (start from zero), reaction time (use multiples or light gates).
- A vector answer needs a size and a direction.
- Pythagoras only works when the two vectors are at 90°. Vectors along one line are simply added or subtracted (see Unit 4).
