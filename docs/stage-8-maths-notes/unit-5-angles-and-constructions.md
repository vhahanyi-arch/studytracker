# Unit 5 — Angles and constructions

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 8, objectives 8Gg.10, 8Gg.11, 8Gg.12 and 8Gp.01. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Angle facts to build on

- Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.
- Angles around a point add up to 360°.
- Angles in a triangle add up to 180°.
- Angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360°.

### The exterior angle of a triangle — 8Gg.10

Extend one side of a triangle. The exterior angle formed equals the sum of the two interior angles opposite it. Reason: the three interior angles a + b + c = 180°, and the exterior angle d and c lie on a straight line, so c + d = 180°. Therefore d = a + b.

### Angles and parallel lines — 8Gg.11

When a line (a transversal) crosses two parallel lines:

- **corresponding angles** are equal (an F shape);
- **alternate angles** are equal (a Z shape);
- **co-interior angles** add up to 180° (a C shape).

Where any two lines cross, **vertically opposite angles** are equal. Give the reason with every angle you find, for example "alternate angles are equal".

### Constructions — 8Gg.12

Use a ruler, compasses and a protractor, and leave the construction arcs visible.

- **Triangle with three sides (SSS):** draw one side. Set the compasses to the second length and draw an arc from one end; set them to the third length and draw an arc from the other end. Join both ends to the point where the arcs cross.
- **Triangle with two sides and the included angle (SAS), or two angles and the included side (ASA):** draw the given side, measure the angles with a protractor, and draw the other sides.
- **Perpendicular bisector of a line segment (and its midpoint):** with the compasses open more than half the length, draw arcs above and below the line from each end. Join the two crossing points. This line is at 90° to the segment and cuts it at its midpoint.
- **Angle bisector:** from the vertex, draw an arc crossing both arms. From each crossing point, draw arcs of equal radius that meet inside the angle. Join the vertex to where they meet.

### Bearings — 8Gp.01

A bearing gives direction as an angle measured **clockwise from north**, written with three figures: east is 090°, south 180°, west 270°, north-east 045°. To find the bearing of B from A, stand at A, face north and turn clockwise until you face B.

The bearing back (of A from B) differs by 180°: add 180° if the bearing is less than 180°, otherwise subtract 180°.

## Key facts and formulas

| Fact | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Exterior angle = sum of the two interior opposite angles | For any triangle | Finding an angle outside a triangle in one step. |
| Corresponding angles equal; alternate angles equal | Two parallel lines and a transversal | Missing angles in parallel-line diagrams. |
| Co-interior angles add to 180° | Between two parallel lines, same side | Same diagrams; also proving lines are parallel. |
| Vertically opposite angles equal | Where two lines cross | Any crossing lines. |
| Bearing: clockwise from north, three figures | 045°, 120°, 300° | Directions on maps and in navigation. |
| Back bearing = bearing ± 180° | The reverse direction | Returning journeys. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: exterior angle of a triangle

**Example.** Two interior angles of a triangle are 48° and 67°. The exterior angle at the third vertex is 48° + 67° = 115°. (The third interior angle is 180° − 115° = 65°.)

**Example — find an interior angle.** An exterior angle is 130° and one interior opposite angle is 55°. The other is 130° − 55° = 75°.

### Situation: parallel lines

**Example — a chain of reasons.** A transversal makes an angle of 70° with the first of two parallel lines. The corresponding angle at the second line is 70° (corresponding angles are equal). The angle next to it on the straight line is 110° (angles on a straight line add to 180°).

**Example — algebra.** Two alternate angles are (2x + 10)° and (3x − 20)°. Alternate angles are equal, so 2x + 10 = 3x − 20, giving x = 30. Each angle is 70°.

**Example — co-interior.** Two co-interior angles are y and 2y. y + 2y = 180°, so y = 60°.

### Situation: constructions

**Example — SSS triangle.** To draw a triangle with sides 7 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm: draw 7 cm, then arcs of radius 5 cm and 6 cm from its two ends. Where they cross is the third vertex.

**Example — midpoint.** To find the midpoint of AB without measuring, construct the perpendicular bisector of AB: where it crosses AB is the midpoint.

**Example — bisect 80°.** The angle bisector of an 80° angle makes two 40° angles. Check with a protractor.

### Situation: bearings

**Example — reading a bearing.** A lighthouse is south-east of a boat: its bearing is 135°.

**Example — back bearing.** B is on a bearing of 070° from A. The bearing of A from B is 070° + 180° = 250°.

**Example — back bearing greater than 180°.** Q is on a bearing of 300° from P. The bearing of P from Q is 300° − 180° = 120°.

## Common mistakes

- Measuring a bearing anticlockwise, or from east.
- Writing a bearing with two figures: 45° should be 045°.
- Using co-interior angles as if they were equal (they add up to 180°).
- Calling angles alternate or corresponding when the lines are not parallel.
- Rubbing out construction arcs: they show the method.
