# Unit 8 — Shapes and symmetry

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 8, objectives 8Gg.01, 8Gg.05, 8Gg.07 and 8Gg.09. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### The family of quadrilaterals — 8Gg.01

Quadrilaterals are four-sided shapes. Some are special cases of others: a shape belongs to every group whose properties it has.

| Shape | Sides | Angles | Diagonals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Square | 4 equal; opposite sides parallel | All 90° | Equal; bisect each other at 90° |
| Rectangle | Opposite sides equal and parallel | All 90° | Equal; bisect each other |
| Rhombus | 4 equal; opposite sides parallel | Opposite angles equal | Bisect each other at 90° |
| Parallelogram | Opposite sides equal and parallel | Opposite angles equal | Bisect each other |
| Kite | Two pairs of equal adjacent sides | One pair of opposite angles equal | Cross at 90°; one bisects the other |
| Trapezium | One pair of parallel sides | — | — |

So a square is a special rectangle (all sides equal) and a special rhombus (all angles 90°). Rectangles and rhombuses are special parallelograms. A rhombus is also a special kite.

### Euler's formula — 8Gg.05

For a polyhedron (a 3D shape with flat faces), the numbers of vertices (corners) V, faces F and edges E are linked by V + F − E = 2. Use it to check counts or find a missing one.

### Views of 3D shapes — 8Gg.07

A 3D shape can be drawn as three 2D views:

- **plan:** the view from directly above;
- **front elevation:** the view from the front;
- **side elevation:** the view from the side.

Hidden edges are not drawn in simple views; a visible edge inside a view is a line.

### Symmetry of regular polygons — 8Gg.09

A regular polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal. A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order n. The order of rotational symmetry is how many times the shape fits onto itself in one full turn.

## Key facts and formulas

| Fact or formula | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| V + F − E = 2 | Euler's formula for polyhedra | Finding a missing count of vertices, faces or edges. |
| Regular n-gon: n lines of symmetry, rotational order n | Symmetry depends only on n | Any regular polygon. |
| Every square is a rectangle and a rhombus | Inclusive families | Classifying quadrilaterals. |
| Plan, front elevation, side elevation | Views from above, front and side | Drawing and recognising 3D shapes. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: classifying a quadrilateral

**Example.** A quadrilateral has four equal sides and diagonals that are not equal. It is a rhombus but not a square (a square's diagonals are equal).

**Example — true or false.** "Every rectangle is a parallelogram" is true. "Every parallelogram is a rectangle" is false: a parallelogram need not have right angles.

### Situation: Euler's formula

**Example — check a cube.** V = 8, F = 6, E = 12: 8 + 6 − 12 = 2.

**Example — triangular prism.** V = 6, F = 5, E = 9: 6 + 5 − 9 = 2.

**Example — find the missing number.** A polyhedron has 12 faces and 30 edges. V + 12 − 30 = 2, so V = 20.

**Example — square-based pyramid.** V = 5, F = 5, so E = 5 + 5 − 2 = 8.

### Situation: drawing views

**Example — cylinder standing upright.** Plan: a circle. Front and side elevations: rectangles.

**Example — square-based pyramid.** Plan: a square with its two diagonals (the sloping edges meet at the top). Front and side elevations: triangles.

**Example — L-shaped prism.** Plan and one elevation are rectangles; the elevation looking along its length shows the L shape.

### Situation: symmetry

**Example — regular hexagon.** 6 lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 6.

**Example — non-regular shapes.** A rectangle (not a square) has 2 lines of symmetry and order 2. A parallelogram (not a rectangle or rhombus) has no lines of symmetry and order 2. A kite has 1 line of symmetry and order 1.

**Example — find n.** A regular polygon has rotational symmetry of order 8: it is a regular octagon with 8 lines of symmetry.

## Common mistakes

- Saying a square is not a rectangle. It is: a rectangle with equal sides.
- Mixing up the order of rotational symmetry with the number of lines of symmetry for non-regular shapes.
- Saying a shape with no rotational symmetry has order 0: every shape has order at least 1.
- Counting the edges of a prism incorrectly; count edges on one end, the other end, and those joining them.
- Using Euler's formula for shapes with curved faces such as cylinders.
