# Unit 15 — Distance, area and volume

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 8, objectives 8Gg.02, 8Gg.03, 8Gg.04, 8Gg.06 and 8Gg.08. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### π and the circumference of a circle — 8Gg.02

For every circle, the circumference divided by the diameter is the same number, π (pi), which is about 3.14. So circumference C = πd, or C = 2πr since the diameter is twice the radius. Use the π key on a calculator and round at the end.

### Miles and kilometres — 8Gg.03

5 miles is about 8 km. Miles to kilometres: divide by 5 and multiply by 8. Kilometres to miles: divide by 8 and multiply by 5.

### Area of parallelograms and trapezia — 8Gg.04

- Parallelogram: area = base × perpendicular height. Cut a triangle off one end and move it to the other: it becomes a rectangle.
- Trapezium: area = ½ × (a + b) × h, where a and b are the parallel sides and h is the perpendicular distance between them. Two identical trapezia make a parallelogram with base a + b.

The height must be perpendicular (at 90°) to the base, not the length of a sloping side.

### Volume of a triangular prism — 8Gg.06

A prism has the same cross-section all along its length. Volume = area of cross-section × length. For a triangular prism the cross-section is a triangle: area = ½ × base × height.

### Surface area — 8Gg.08

Surface area is the total area of all the faces. Sketch the net and add the area of each face.

- Cube with edge s: 6s².
- Cuboid: 2(lw + lh + wh).
- Triangular prism: two triangles plus three rectangles.
- Pyramid: the base plus each triangular face (use the slant height of each triangle).

## Key facts and formulas

| Formula | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| C = πd = 2πr | Circumference of a circle | Perimeters of circles and parts of circles. |
| 5 miles ≈ 8 km | Conversion between units of distance | Road distances and speeds. |
| A = b × h | Area of a parallelogram (perpendicular height) | Parallelograms. |
| A = ½(a + b)h | Area of a trapezium | Trapezia. |
| V = area of cross-section × length | Volume of a prism | Triangular prisms (and all prisms). |
| Cube 6s²; cuboid 2(lw + lh + wh) | Surface area | Boxes, packaging, painting. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: circumference

**Example.** A wheel has diameter 60 cm. Circumference = π × 60 = 188.5 cm (1 d.p.).

**Example — find the diameter.** A circle has circumference 50 cm. d = 50 ÷ π = 15.9 cm.

**Example — semicircle perimeter.** A semicircle has diameter 12 cm. Perimeter = curved part + straight edge = π × 12 ÷ 2 + 12 = 18.85 + 12 = 30.85 cm.

### Situation: miles and kilometres

**Example.** 40 miles = 40 ÷ 5 × 8 = 64 km. 100 km = 100 ÷ 8 × 5 = 62.5 miles.

**Example — speed.** A speed limit of 50 mph is about 50 ÷ 5 × 8 = 80 km/h.

### Situation: parallelograms and trapezia

**Example — parallelogram.** Base 9 cm, perpendicular height 4 cm, sloping side 5 cm. Area = 9 × 4 = 36 cm² (the 5 cm is not used).

**Example — trapezium.** Parallel sides 6 cm and 10 cm, height 5 cm. Area = ½ × (6 + 10) × 5 = 40 cm².

**Example — find a length.** A trapezium has area 54 cm², height 6 cm and one parallel side 7 cm. ½ × (7 + b) × 6 = 54, so 7 + b = 18 and b = 11 cm.

### Situation: volume of a triangular prism

**Example.** A prism has a triangular cross-section with base 6 cm and height 4 cm, and length 10 cm. Cross-section area = ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm². Volume = 12 × 10 = 120 cm³.

### Situation: surface area

**Example — cuboid.** 5 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm: 2(15 + 10 + 6) = 2 × 31 = 62 cm².

**Example — triangular prism.** A right-angled triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm, prism length 10 cm. Two triangles: 2 × ½ × 3 × 4 = 12 cm². Three rectangles: (3 + 4 + 5) × 10 = 120 cm². Total = 132 cm².

**Example — square-based pyramid.** Base 6 cm by 6 cm; each triangular face has slant height 5 cm. Base 36 cm² + 4 × ½ × 6 × 5 = 36 + 60 = 96 cm².

## Common mistakes

- Using the radius in C = πd, or the diameter in C = 2πr.
- Using the sloping side of a parallelogram instead of its perpendicular height.
- Forgetting the ½ in the trapezium or triangle formula.
- Giving surface area in cm³ or volume in cm².
- Missing the hidden faces when finding surface area: count all faces of the net.
