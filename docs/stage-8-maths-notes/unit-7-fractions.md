# Unit 7 — Fractions

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 8, objectives 8Nf.01, 8Nf.02 and 8Nf.03. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Writing fractions and mixed numbers

A mixed number such as 2 1/3 means 2 + 1/3. As an improper fraction, multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator: 2 1/3 = 7/3. To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by their highest common factor: 18/24 = 3/4.

### Recurring decimals and fractions — 8Nf.01

Dividing the numerator by the denominator turns a fraction into a decimal. Some decimals stop (1/4 = 0.25); others repeat for ever. Learn these:

- 1/3 = 0.333..., 2/3 = 0.666...
- 1/9 = 0.111..., 2/9 = 0.222..., and so on: n/9 = 0.nnn...
- 1/6 = 0.1666..., 5/6 = 0.8333...

To change a recurring decimal to a fraction, call it x, multiply by 10 (one repeating digit) or 100 (two repeating digits), and subtract to remove the repeating part.

### Subtracting mixed numbers — 8Nf.02

Estimate first using the whole numbers. Then either:

- change both to improper fractions, use a common denominator, subtract, and change back to a mixed number; or
- subtract the whole numbers and the fractions separately. If the second fraction is bigger, borrow 1 from the whole number first.

Give the answer as a mixed number in its simplest form.

### Multiplying and dividing with integers — 8Nf.03

- **Integer × mixed number:** multiply the whole number and the fraction separately and add (3 × 2 2/5 = 6 + 6/5), or change to an improper fraction first.
- **Integer ÷ proper fraction:** ask "how many of this fraction fit into the integer?". Multiply by the reciprocal (flip the fraction): 6 ÷ 2/3 = 6 × 3/2 = 9.

Dividing by a fraction less than 1 gives an answer bigger than the integer.

## Key facts and formulas

| Fact or method | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| a b/c = (a × c + b)/c | Mixed to improper fraction | Before multiplying, dividing or subtracting. |
| a/c − b/d: use a common denominator | Make the pieces the same size | Adding and subtracting fractions. |
| n ÷ (a/b) = n × (b/a) | Divide by a fraction = multiply by its reciprocal | Integer ÷ proper fraction. |
| n/9 = 0.nnn... | Ninths recur | Recognising recurring decimals. |
| x = 0.aaa...: 10x − x = a | Remove the repeating part | Recurring decimal to fraction. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: recurring decimal to fraction

**Example — one repeating digit.** x = 0.777... Then 10x = 7.777... Subtract: 9x = 7, so x = 7/9.

**Example — two repeating digits.** x = 0.454545... Then 100x = 45.4545... Subtract: 99x = 45, so x = 45/99 = 5/11.

**Example — fraction to decimal.** 5/6 = 5 ÷ 6 = 0.8333...

### Situation: subtracting mixed numbers

**Example — improper fractions.** 4 1/3 − 1 3/4. Estimate: about 4 − 2 = 2.

- 4 1/3 = 13/3 = 52/12 and 1 3/4 = 7/4 = 21/12.
- 52/12 − 21/12 = 31/12 = 2 7/12.

**Example — borrowing.** 5 1/4 − 2 1/2: 1/4 is smaller than 1/2, so write 5 1/4 as 4 5/4. Then 4 5/4 − 2 2/4 = 2 3/4.

**Example — in context.** A plank is 6 1/2 m long and 2 5/6 m is cut off. 6 1/2 − 2 5/6 = 39/6 − 17/6 = 22/6 = 3 2/3 m is left.

### Situation: integer × mixed number

**Example.** 3 × 2 2/5 = 3 × 2 + 3 × 2/5 = 6 + 6/5 = 6 + 1 1/5 = 7 1/5.

**Example — recipe.** A recipe needs 1 3/4 cups of flour per cake. For 4 cakes: 4 × 1 3/4 = 4 + 3 = 7 cups.

### Situation: integer ÷ proper fraction

**Example.** 6 ÷ 2/3 = 6 × 3/2 = 18/2 = 9. (Nine two-thirds make 6.)

**Example — not a whole number.** 5 ÷ 3/4 = 5 × 4/3 = 20/3 = 6 2/3.

**Example — in context.** How many 1/4 kg bags can be filled from 7 kg of rice? 7 ÷ 1/4 = 28 bags.

## Common mistakes

- Subtracting the numerators and denominators separately: 3/4 − 1/3 is not 2/1.
- Forgetting to borrow when the second fraction is bigger.
- Multiplying only the whole part: 3 × 2 2/5 is not 6 2/5.
- Dividing by a fraction without flipping it.
- Leaving answers as improper fractions or not simplified when a mixed number in simplest form is asked for.
