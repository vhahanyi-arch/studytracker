# Unit 12 — Ratio and proportion

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 8, objectives 8Nf.09, 8Nf.10 and 8Nf.11. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Ratio and direct proportion — 8Nf.09

A ratio compares parts of a whole: 2 : 3 means for every 2 of the first there are 3 of the second. Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when they increase at the same rate: double one and the other doubles. Ratios describe proportion: if a recipe uses flour and sugar in the ratio 3 : 1, any amount of the mixture keeps that ratio.

The **unitary method** solves direct-proportion problems: first find the value of one unit, then multiply.

### Simplifying and comparing ratios — 8Nf.10

Simplify a ratio by dividing all parts by their highest common factor: 18 : 24 = 3 : 4. If the parts have different units, change them to the same unit first and then drop the units. To compare ratios, write each in the form 1 : n (divide by the first part) or n : 1.

### Sharing in a ratio — 8Nf.11

To divide an amount in a ratio:

- add the parts to find the total number of parts;
- divide the amount by the total to find one part;
- multiply each ratio number by one part.

Check that the shares add up to the original amount. If you are given one share (or the difference between shares) instead of the total, use it to find one part.

## Key facts and formulas

| Fact or method | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| a : b = ka : kb | Multiply or divide all parts by the same number | Simplifying or scaling ratios. |
| Same units before simplifying | 40 cm : 2 m = 40 : 200 | Ratios with mixed units. |
| One part = amount ÷ total parts | The value of one share | Sharing in a ratio. |
| Unitary method: find one, then scale | Direct proportion | Recipes, prices, rates. |
| 1 : n form | Divide both by the first part | Comparing ratios. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: simplifying ratios

**Example.** 18 : 24 = 3 : 4 (divide by 6). 15 : 25 : 40 = 3 : 5 : 8 (divide by 5).

**Example — different units.** 40 cm : 2 m = 40 cm : 200 cm = 1 : 5. 750 g : 1.5 kg = 750 : 1500 = 1 : 2.

### Situation: comparing ratios

**Example.** Squash A is mixed as 2 : 7 (squash : water) and B as 3 : 10. In the form 1 : n: A is 1 : 3.5 and B is 1 : 3.33... B has less water for each part of squash, so B is stronger.

### Situation: sharing an amount

**Example — three parts.** Share $150 in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5. Total parts = 10; one part = $15. Shares: $30, $45 and $75 (check: 30 + 45 + 75 = 150).

**Example — given one share.** Sweets are shared in the ratio 3 : 5 and the larger share is 40. One part = 40 ÷ 5 = 8. The smaller share is 24 and the total is 64.

**Example — given the difference.** Two numbers are in the ratio 2 : 7 and differ by 35. The difference is 5 parts, so one part = 7. The numbers are 14 and 49.

### Situation: direct proportion (unitary method)

**Example — prices.** 5 pens cost $3.50. One pen costs $0.70, so 8 pens cost $5.60.

**Example — recipe.** A recipe for 4 people uses 300 g of flour. For 1 person, 75 g; for 6 people, 450 g.

**Example — best buy.** 500 g of cereal costs $2.40 and 750 g costs $3.45. Price per 100 g: 2.40 ÷ 5 = $0.48 and 3.45 ÷ 7.5 = $0.46. The 750 g box is better value.

## Common mistakes

- Simplifying a ratio with different units without converting first.
- Dividing an amount by one of the ratio numbers instead of by the total number of parts.
- Writing the ratio in the wrong order: "boys to girls" means boys first.
- Treating the ratio 3 : 5 as the fraction 3/5 of the whole (it is 3/8 of the whole).
