# Unit 10 — Percentages

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 8, objective 8Nf.05. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Percentages as decimals

Per cent means "out of 100": 35% = 35/100 = 0.35. To find a percentage of an amount, multiply by the decimal: 35% of 80 = 0.35 × 80 = 28. To write one amount as a percentage of another, divide and multiply by 100: 18 out of 24 = 18 ÷ 24 × 100 = 75%.

### Percentage increase and decrease — 8Nf.05

Use a multiplier: the new amount as a decimal of the original.

- Increase by r%: multiply by (1 + r/100). An increase of 15% means × 1.15.
- Decrease by r%: multiply by (1 − r/100). A decrease of 20% means × 0.80.

The **absolute change** is the actual amount added or taken away (in dollars, people, kilograms). The **percentage change** compares that change with the original amount:

percentage change = (change ÷ original amount) × 100.

Always divide by the original amount, not the new one.

## Key facts and formulas

| Formula | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| r% of A = (r/100) × A | Percentage of an amount | Discounts, tax, tips. |
| New = original × (1 + r/100) | Increase by r% | Price rises, pay rises, population growth. |
| New = original × (1 − r/100) | Decrease by r% | Sales, depreciation, reductions. |
| Absolute change = new − original | The actual difference | Saying "how much more" or "how much less". |
| Percentage change = change ÷ original × 100 | Change relative to the start | Comparing changes of different sizes. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: increasing an amount

**Example.** A $80 jacket's price rises by 15%. New price = 80 × 1.15 = $92. The absolute change is $12.

**Example — two steps.** 15% of 80 = 12, and 80 + 12 = 92. The multiplier does it in one step.

### Situation: decreasing an amount

**Example.** A town's population of 2400 falls by 12%. New population = 2400 × 0.88 = 2112. The absolute change is 288 people.

**Example — a sale.** A $45 game has 30% off: 45 × 0.70 = $31.50.

### Situation: finding the percentage change

**Example — increase.** A plant grows from 50 cm to 62 cm. Change = 12 cm. Percentage change = 12 ÷ 50 × 100 = 24% increase.

**Example — decrease.** A car's value falls from $16 000 to $12 000. Change = $4 000. Percentage decrease = 4 000 ÷ 16 000 × 100 = 25%.

### Situation: comparing absolute and percentage change

**Example.** Shop A raises a price from $10 to $12; shop B from $100 to $105.

- Absolute changes: A $2, B $5, so B's price rose by more money.
- Percentage changes: A 2 ÷ 10 × 100 = 20%; B 5 ÷ 100 × 100 = 5%, so A's price rose by a greater proportion.

### Situation: finding the multiplier

**Example.** A price changes from $60 to $51. Multiplier = 51 ÷ 60 = 0.85, which is a 15% decrease.

## Common mistakes

- Dividing by the new amount when finding a percentage change.
- Using × 0.2 instead of × 0.8 for a 20% decrease (× 0.2 gives the amount taken off, not the new amount).
- Adding percentages of different amounts as if they were the same.
- Confusing a change of "5%" with a change of "5 units".
