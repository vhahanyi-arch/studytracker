# Unit 14 — Position and transformation

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 8, objectives 8Gp.02, 8Gp.03, 8Gp.04, 8Gp.05 and 8Gp.06. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Midpoint of a line segment — 8Gp.02

The midpoint is halfway between the two ends. Its x-coordinate is the mean of the two x-coordinates, and its y-coordinate is the mean of the two y-coordinates.

### Translation — 8Gp.03

A translation slides a shape without turning or flipping it. It is described by a column vector: the top number is the movement right (negative: left) and the bottom number is the movement up (negative: down). In these notes the vector with top 3 and bottom −2 is written (3, −2): 3 right and 2 down. The image is congruent to the object: same shape and size.

### Reflection — 8Gp.04

A reflection flips a shape in a mirror line. Each point of the image is the same distance from the mirror line as the object point, measured at right angles to the line. Mirror lines in this stage:

- vertical lines x = a and horizontal lines y = b;
- the diagonal line y = x, which swaps the coordinates: (a, b) becomes (b, a);
- the diagonal line y = −x: (a, b) becomes (−b, −a).

### Rotation — 8Gp.05

A rotation turns a shape about a fixed point, the centre of rotation. Describe it with the centre, the angle and the direction (clockwise or anticlockwise). Tracing paper helps: trace the shape, hold the centre with a pencil point, and turn. About the origin:

- 90° clockwise: (x, y) becomes (y, −x);
- 90° anticlockwise: (x, y) becomes (−y, x);
- 180°: (x, y) becomes (−x, −y).

### Enlargement — 8Gp.06

An enlargement changes the size of a shape but keeps its shape: angles stay the same and every length is multiplied by the scale factor. From a centre of enlargement, multiply the distance (horizontally and vertically) from the centre to each point by the scale factor. The image is similar to the object, not congruent (unless the scale factor is 1).

## Key facts and formulas

| Formula | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2) | Mean of the coordinates | Halfway between two points. |
| Translation by (a, b): (x, y) becomes (x + a, y + b) | Move a across, b up | Translating shapes and points. |
| Reflect in y = x: (a, b) becomes (b, a) | Swap the coordinates | Diagonal mirror line. |
| Rotate 180° about the origin: (x, y) becomes (−x, −y) | Change both signs | Half turns. |
| Enlargement: image distance from centre = scale factor × object distance | Lengths multiply | Drawing enlargements. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: midpoints

**Example.** The midpoint of (2, 5) and (8, −1) is ((2 + 8)/2, (5 + (−1))/2) = (5, 2).

**Example — find an end point.** M(4, 3) is the midpoint of A(1, 7) and B. From A to M is 3 right and 4 down, so B is another 3 right and 4 down: B = (7, −1).

### Situation: translation

**Example.** Translate the triangle with vertices (1, 2), (3, 2), (1, 5) by (4, −3): the image is (5, −1), (7, −1), (5, 2).

**Example — find the vector.** A point moves from (−2, 6) to (3, 1). The vector is (5, −5): 5 right and 5 down.

### Situation: reflection

**Example — vertical line.** Reflect (5, 1) in x = 2: it is 3 to the right of the line, so the image is 3 to the left: (−1, 1).

**Example — horizontal line.** Reflect (3, 4) in y = −1: it is 5 above the line, so the image is 5 below: (3, −6).

**Example — diagonal lines.** Reflect (2, 7) in y = x: (7, 2). Reflect (2, 7) in y = −x: (−7, −2).

### Situation: rotation

**Example.** Rotate (2, 5) through 90° clockwise about the origin: (5, −2). Through 180°: (−2, −5).

**Example — describe a rotation.** A flag pointing up from the origin ends up pointing right. That is a rotation of 90° clockwise about (0, 0) (or 270° anticlockwise).

### Situation: enlargement

**Example.** Enlarge the point (3, 2) by scale factor 3, centre (1, 1). From the centre it is 2 across and 1 up. Multiply by 3: 6 across and 3 up. The image is (7, 4).

**Example — lengths.** A rectangle 2 cm by 3 cm enlarged by scale factor 4 becomes 8 cm by 12 cm. Its angles are still 90°.

## Common mistakes

- Reading a column vector's top number as up and bottom number as across.
- Measuring reflection distances along the grid instead of at right angles to a diagonal mirror line.
- Leaving out the centre or the direction when describing a rotation.
- Enlarging from the origin when another centre is given.
- Calling an enlargement congruent: only translations, reflections and rotations give congruent images.
