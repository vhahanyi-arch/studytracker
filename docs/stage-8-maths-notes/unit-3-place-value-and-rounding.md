# Unit 3 — Place value and rounding

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 8, objectives 8Np.01 and 8Np.02. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Multiplying and dividing by 0.1 and 0.01 — 8Np.01

0.1 is one tenth and 0.01 is one hundredth, so:

- multiplying by 0.1 is the same as dividing by 10;
- dividing by 0.1 is the same as multiplying by 10;
- multiplying by 0.01 is the same as dividing by 100;
- dividing by 0.01 is the same as multiplying by 100.

The digits move along the place-value columns; they do not change. Multiplying by a number less than 1 makes a positive number smaller; dividing by a number less than 1 makes it larger. The same rules work for negative numbers and decimals.

### Significant figures — 8Np.02

The first significant figure is the first non-zero digit, reading from the left. After that, every digit counts, including zeros.

- In 0.004 637, the first significant figure is 4 (the leading zeros are not significant).
- In 30 512, the zero between 3 and 5 is significant.

To round to n significant figures: find the nth significant figure, look at the next digit, and round up if it is 5 or more. Then keep the size of the number: fill places before the decimal point with zeros, and drop digits after it.

Rounding to significant figures is useful for estimating: round each number to 1 significant figure and calculate in your head.

## Key facts and formulas

| Fact or method | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| × 0.1 = ÷ 10; ÷ 0.1 = × 10 | Digits move one place | Calculations with tenths. |
| × 0.01 = ÷ 100; ÷ 0.01 = × 100 | Digits move two places | Calculations with hundredths. |
| First significant figure = first non-zero digit | Leading zeros never count | Rounding very small numbers. |
| Keep the place value when rounding | 34 572 to 2 s.f. is 35 000, not 35 | Rounding large numbers. |
| Estimate = round each number to 1 s.f. | A quick check of size | Before or after any calculation. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: multiplying and dividing by 0.1 and 0.01

**Example — tenths.** 45 × 0.1 = 4.5. 3.6 ÷ 0.1 = 36. −2.7 × 0.1 = −0.27.

**Example — hundredths.** 7.2 × 0.01 = 0.072. 0.58 ÷ 0.01 = 58. −30 × 0.01 = −0.3.

**Example — find the missing number.** 6.4 × ? = 0.064: the answer is 100 times smaller, so ? = 0.01.

**Example — in context.** A sheet of card is 0.01 m thick. A stack 0.85 m high has 0.85 ÷ 0.01 = 85 sheets.

### Situation: rounding large numbers

**Example.** 34 572 to 2 significant figures: the 2nd significant figure is 4 and the next digit is 5, so round up to 35 000. To 1 significant figure: 30 000.

**Example — rounding up past a 9.** 899 to 1 significant figure is 900. 2 960 to 2 significant figures is 3 000 (write 3 000; the second significant figure is the zero after 3).

### Situation: rounding small numbers

**Example.** 0.004 637 to 2 significant figures: the significant figures start at 4; 4 and 6, and the next digit is 3, so 0.0046.

**Example — keep the final zero.** 0.0998 to 2 significant figures is 0.10. The zero shows the answer is accurate to 2 significant figures.

**Example — decimals.** 3.0496 to 3 significant figures: 3, 0 and 4, next digit 9, so 3.05.

### Situation: estimating

**Example.** Estimate 6.8 × 41.3 ÷ 0.48. To 1 significant figure: 7 × 40 ÷ 0.5 = 280 ÷ 0.5 = 560. (The exact answer is about 585, so the estimate shows the size is right.)

**Example — check a calculator answer.** A student gets 2 443.6 for 59.6 × 4.1. Estimate: 60 × 4 = 240. The student's answer is ten times too big; the correct answer is 244.36.

### Situation: choosing a sensible accuracy

**Example.** A hall's area is calculated as 245.736 m². Measurements were only to the nearest metre, so give the answer to 2 or 3 significant figures: 250 m² or 246 m².

## Common mistakes

- Thinking × 0.1 makes a number bigger. Multiplying by 0.1 divides by 10.
- Counting leading zeros as significant figures: 0.0046 has 2 significant figures, not 4.
- Dropping the place value: 34 572 to 2 s.f. is 35 000, not 35.
- Dropping a significant zero: 0.0998 to 2 s.f. is 0.10, not 0.1.
- Rounding in stages (rounding 0.0449 to 0.045 and then to 0.05). Always round the original number once: 0.0449 to 1 s.f. is 0.04.
