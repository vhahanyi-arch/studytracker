# Unit 4 — Decimals

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 8, objectives 8Nf.04, 8Nf.06, 8Nf.07 and 8Nf.08. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Laws of arithmetic and order of operations — 8Nf.04

The laws of arithmetic let you rearrange a calculation to make it easier, with decimals and fractions as well as whole numbers:

- **commutative:** order does not matter for × and +, so 0.25 × 7.3 × 4 = 0.25 × 4 × 7.3 = 7.3;
- **associative:** grouping does not matter for × and +;
- **distributive:** a × b + a × c = a × (b + c), so 3.7 × 9 + 3.7 × 1 = 3.7 × 10 = 37.

Subtraction and division are not commutative: 5 − 2 is not 2 − 5. Follow BIDMAS when you do not rearrange.

### Comparing and ordering — 8Nf.06

To compare decimals, line up the decimal points and compare digit by digit from the left; add zeros so they have the same number of places. To compare fractions with decimals, change the fractions to decimals.

For negative numbers, the one further from zero is smaller: −0.4 < −0.35, because −0.4 is further left on the number line.

Symbols: = equal, ≠ not equal, < less than, > greater than, ≤ less than or equal to, ≥ greater than or equal to.

### Multiplying decimals — 8Nf.07

Estimate first. Then ignore the decimal points, multiply the whole numbers, and put the decimal point back so the answer has as many decimal places as the two numbers together. Check against the estimate.

Multiplying by a number between 0 and 1 makes a positive number smaller.

### Dividing by a decimal — 8Nf.08

Make the divisor a whole number by multiplying both numbers by 10 (for one decimal place). The answer does not change, because both are scaled by the same amount: 7.56 ÷ 0.6 = 75.6 ÷ 6. Then divide as usual. Dividing by a number between 0 and 1 makes a positive number larger.

## Key facts and formulas

| Fact or method | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| a × b = b × a; a + b = b + a | Commutative law | Reordering to make easy pairs (0.25 × 4 = 1). |
| a × b + a × c = a × (b + c) | Distributive law | Spotting a common factor. |
| Decimal places in answer = decimal places in both numbers together | For multiplication | Placing the decimal point. |
| a ÷ b = (10a) ÷ (10b) | Scale both numbers | Dividing by a 1-decimal-place number. |
| −0.4 < −0.35 | Further from zero is smaller for negatives | Ordering negative decimals. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: using the laws to calculate mentally

**Example — reorder.** 0.25 × 7.3 × 4 = (0.25 × 4) × 7.3 = 1 × 7.3 = 7.3.

**Example — distributive.** 3.7 × 9 + 3.7 × 1 = 3.7 × (9 + 1) = 37.

**Example — split a number.** 4.8 × 25 = 4.8 × 100 ÷ 4 = 480 ÷ 4 = 120.

**Example — order of operations.** 1/2 + 0.3 × 4 = 0.5 + 1.2 = 1.7 (multiply first).

### Situation: comparing and ordering

**Example — two decimals.** Which is bigger, 0.38 or 3/8? 3/8 = 0.375, and 0.380 > 0.375, so 0.38 > 3/8.

**Example — order a list.** Put in order, smallest first: 0.05, −0.75, 1/4, −1.2, −3/5. As decimals: 0.05, −0.75, 0.25, −1.2, −0.6. Order: −1.2, −0.75, −3/5, 0.05, 1/4.

**Example — symbols.** −2.5 < −2.05; 0.7 ≠ 0.07; 4/5 ≥ 0.8 is true (they are equal).

### Situation: multiplying decimals

**Example — decimal by decimal.** 3.6 × 0.24. Estimate: 4 × 0.2 = 0.8. Work out 36 × 24 = 864. Decimal places: 1 + 2 = 3, so 0.864.

**Example — decimal by integer.** 2.7 × 13: 27 × 13 = 351, one decimal place, so 35.1. Estimate 3 × 13 = 39.

**Example — money.** 2.4 m of ribbon at $3.75 per metre: 24 × 375 = 9000, with 1 + 2 = 3 decimal places, so 9.000 = $9.00.

### Situation: dividing by a decimal

**Example.** 7.56 ÷ 0.6 = 75.6 ÷ 6 = 12.6. Check: 12.6 × 0.6 = 7.56.

**Example — answer bigger than the number.** 15 ÷ 0.4 = 150 ÷ 4 = 37.5.

**Example — how many fit.** How many 0.3 litre glasses can be filled from 5.4 litres? 5.4 ÷ 0.3 = 54 ÷ 3 = 18 glasses.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking 0.38 < 0.375 because "375 is bigger than 38". Line up the digits first.
- Ordering negatives the wrong way: −0.75 is smaller than −0.6.
- Putting the decimal point in the wrong place: 0.3 × 0.2 = 0.06, not 0.6.
- Multiplying only one number by 10 when dividing by a decimal.
- Not estimating, so a result ten times too large or small goes unnoticed.
