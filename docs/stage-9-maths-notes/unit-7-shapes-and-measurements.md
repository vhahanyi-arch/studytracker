# Unit 7 — Shapes and measurements

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 9, objectives 9Gg.01, 9Gg.02, 9Gg.03 and 9Gg.10. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Area and circumference of a circle — 9Gg.01

- Circumference C = πd = 2πr.
- Area A = πr².

Check whether you are given the radius or the diameter: r = d ÷ 2. For a semicircle use half the area; for a quarter circle, a quarter. The perimeter of a sector-shaped piece includes the straight edges as well as the curved part.

### Very small and very large units — 9Gg.02

| Measure | Units and conversions |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 km = 1 000 m; 1 m = 100 cm = 1 000 mm; 1 mm = 1 000 micrometres (μm); 1 μm = 1 000 nanometres (nm) |
| Mass | 1 tonne (t) = 1 000 kg; 1 kg = 1 000 g; 1 g = 1 000 mg; 1 mg = 1 000 micrograms (μg) |
| Capacity | 1 kilolitre (kl) = 1 000 l; 1 l = 1 000 ml; 1 megalitre (Ml) = 1 000 000 l; 1 cm³ = 1 ml |

The prefix tells you the size: kilo = 1 000, mega = 1 000 000, milli = 1/1 000, micro = 1/1 000 000, nano = 1/1 000 000 000. To change to a smaller unit multiply; to a larger unit divide.

### Compound shapes — 9Gg.03

Split a compound shape into rectangles, triangles, circles and parts of circles. Find each area and add (or subtract the area of a hole). Work out any missing lengths from the lengths you know first.

### Pythagoras' theorem — 9Gg.10

In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse c (the longest side, opposite the right angle) and shorter sides a and b:

a² + b² = c².

- To find the hypotenuse: add the squares, then square-root.
- To find a shorter side: subtract the squares, then square-root.
- If a² + b² = c² for a triangle's sides, the triangle has a right angle.

## Key facts and formulas

| Formula | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| C = πd = 2πr | Circumference | Perimeters involving circles. |
| A = πr² | Area of a circle | Circles, semicircles, quarter circles. |
| a² + b² = c² | Pythagoras' theorem (c is the hypotenuse) | Right-angled triangles; distances between points. |
| Distance between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²) | Pythagoras on a grid | Length of a line segment from coordinates. |
| Smaller unit: multiply; larger unit: divide | Unit conversions | Changing between units of measure. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: circle area and circumference

**Example — area from a diameter.** A circle has diameter 10 cm, so r = 5 cm. A = π × 5² = 78.5 cm² (1 d.p.).

**Example — radius from area.** A circle has area 200 cm². r² = 200 ÷ π = 63.66, so r = 7.98 cm.

**Example — semicircle.** A semicircle has radius 6 cm. Area = ½ × π × 36 = 56.5 cm². Perimeter = ½ × 2π × 6 + 12 = 18.85 + 12 = 30.8 cm.

### Situation: converting units

**Example — small lengths.** A human hair is 0.08 mm wide. In micrometres: 0.08 × 1 000 = 80 μm.

**Example — large masses.** A lorry carries 7 500 kg = 7.5 tonnes.

**Example — capacity.** A pool holds 2.5 megalitres = 2 500 000 litres = 2 500 kilolitres.

**Example — tiny masses.** A tablet contains 400 μg of a vitamin = 0.4 mg.

### Situation: compound area

**Example — rectangle with a semicircle.** A shape is a 10 cm by 6 cm rectangle with a semicircle on one 6 cm side. Rectangle = 60 cm²; semicircle radius 3 cm: ½ × π × 9 = 14.14 cm². Total = 74.1 cm².

**Example — subtract a hole.** A 20 cm square tile has a circular hole of radius 4 cm. Area = 400 − π × 16 = 400 − 50.27 = 349.7 cm².

### Situation: Pythagoras — finding the hypotenuse

**Example.** Shorter sides 6 cm and 8 cm: c² = 36 + 64 = 100, so c = 10 cm.

**Example — a ladder.** A ladder reaches 4 m up a wall with its foot 1.5 m from the wall: length² = 16 + 2.25 = 18.25, so length = 4.27 m.

### Situation: Pythagoras — finding a shorter side

**Example.** Hypotenuse 13 cm and one side 5 cm: b² = 169 − 25 = 144, so b = 12 cm.

### Situation: distance between two points

**Example.** From (1, 2) to (7, 10): across 6, up 8. Distance = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10 units.

### Situation: is it a right angle?

**Example.** A triangle has sides 9, 12 and 15 cm. 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225 = 15², so it is right-angled. Sides 7, 8 and 11: 49 + 64 = 113, not 121, so it is not.

## Common mistakes

- Using the diameter in A = πr².
- Squaring after multiplying by π: πr² means π × (r²), not (πr)².
- Adding the squares when finding a shorter side (you must subtract).
- Forgetting to square-root at the end of a Pythagoras calculation.
- Converting area or volume units with the length factor (see Unit 14).
