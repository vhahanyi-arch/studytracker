# Unit 13 — Position and transformation

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 9, objectives 9Gp.02, 9Gp.03, 9Gp.04, 9Gp.05, 9Gp.06 and 9Gp.07. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Points on a line segment — 9Gp.02

To find a point part of the way along a line segment from A to B, find the movement from A to B (across and up), take that fraction of it, and add it to A. The midpoint is the half-way case: the mean of the coordinates.

### Combining transformations — 9Gp.03

Do one transformation, then apply the next to the image. The order can matter. Some combinations equal a single transformation:

- reflection in the x-axis then the y-axis = rotation of 180° about the origin;
- reflections in two parallel lines = a translation;
- two translations = one translation (add the vectors).

### Describing a transformation fully — 9Gp.04

| Transformation | What to state |
|---|---|
| Translation | The vector (across, up) |
| Reflection | The equation of the mirror line |
| Rotation | The centre, the angle and the direction |
| Enlargement | The centre and the scale factor |

### Congruent images — 9Gp.05

Translations, reflections and rotations keep lengths and angles the same, so the image is congruent to the object. Only its position (and possibly orientation) changes.

### Enlargement from any centre — 9Gp.06

The centre of enlargement can be inside the shape, on its edge, or outside it. For each point, multiply its distance from the centre (across and up) by the scale factor. To find the centre from a diagram, draw lines through corresponding points of the object and image: they meet at the centre.

### Enlargement, perimeter and area — 9Gp.07

When a shape is enlarged by scale factor k:

- every length, and so the perimeter, is multiplied by k;
- the area is multiplied by k².

## Key facts and formulas

| Fact or formula | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Point 1/n of the way from A to B = A + 1/n of the movement A to B | Fraction of a line segment | Dividing segments. |
| Translation (a, b) then (c, d) = translation (a + c, b + d) | Add the vectors | Combined translations. |
| Reflect in x-axis then y-axis = rotate 180° about origin | Single equivalent | Combined reflections. |
| Lengths and perimeter × k | Enlargement scale factor k | Perimeters of enlarged shapes. |
| Area × k² | Enlargement scale factor k | Areas of enlarged shapes. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: a point part of the way along a segment

**Example — one third.** A(1, 2) and B(7, 11). From A to B: 6 across, 9 up. One third of that is 2 across, 3 up. The point is (3, 5).

**Example — midpoint.** The midpoint of (−3, 4) and (5, 10) is (1, 7).

### Situation: combining transformations

**Example — two reflections.** Reflect P(3, 2) in the x-axis: (3, −2). Then in the y-axis: (−3, −2). That is the same as rotating (3, 2) by 180° about the origin.

**Example — two translations.** Translating by (2, −1) then by (3, 4) is one translation by (5, 3).

**Example — order matters.** Rotate (1, 0) by 90° anticlockwise about the origin: (0, 1). Then reflect in x = 2: (4, 1). Doing the reflection first gives (3, 0), then the rotation gives (0, 3): a different point.

### Situation: describing a single transformation

**Example.** Triangle A with vertices (1, 1), (3, 1), (1, 2) maps to (−1, 1), (−3, 1), (−1, 2). Each x-coordinate changes sign and y stays the same: a reflection in the y-axis (the line x = 0).

### Situation: enlargement from a centre outside the shape

**Example.** Enlarge the point (4, 3) by scale factor 2 from centre (2, 1). From the centre: 2 across, 2 up. Doubled: 4 across, 4 up. The image is (6, 5).

**Example — centre inside the shape.** With the centre inside, the image surrounds the original shape.

### Situation: perimeter and area after enlargement

**Example.** A 3 cm by 5 cm rectangle is enlarged by scale factor 4. New size 12 cm by 20 cm. Perimeter 16 cm becomes 64 cm (× 4). Area 15 cm² becomes 240 cm² (× 16 = 4²).

**Example — reverse.** A square's area becomes 9 times larger after an enlargement. The scale factor is √9 = 3, so its perimeter becomes 3 times larger.

## Common mistakes

- Describing a rotation without its centre or direction, or a reflection without its line.
- Doing combined transformations in the wrong order.
- Multiplying the area by k instead of k².
- Measuring enlargement distances from the origin when the centre is elsewhere.
- Calling an enlarged image congruent: it is similar.
