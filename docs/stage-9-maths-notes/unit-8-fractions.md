# Unit 8 — Fractions

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 9, objectives 9Nf.01, 9Nf.02, 9Nf.03 and 9Nf.04. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Terminating or recurring? — 9Nf.01

Write the fraction in its simplest form, then look at the prime factors of the denominator:

- if they are only 2s and 5s, the decimal **terminates** (stops), because the denominator divides into a power of 10;
- if there is any other prime factor (3, 7, 11, …), the decimal **recurs**.

Always simplify first: 9/15 = 3/5, which terminates.

### Adding and subtracting fractions and mixed numbers — 9Nf.02

Find a common denominator (the lowest common multiple of the denominators), convert, then add or subtract the numerators. With mixed numbers, either change them to improper fractions or deal with whole numbers and fractions separately (borrowing 1 when needed). Give answers in simplest form, as a mixed number where appropriate (written like 2 5/12).

### Multiplying and dividing fractions — 9Nf.03

- **Multiply:** change mixed numbers to improper fractions, then multiply numerators and multiply denominators. Cancel common factors before multiplying to keep numbers small.
- **Divide:** multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction (flip it). Then cancel and multiply.

### Using the laws of arithmetic — 9Nf.04

The commutative, associative and distributive laws can make calculations with fractions and decimals simpler. Change a decimal to a fraction when that cancels neatly (0.75 = 3/4), or regroup so that pairs make whole numbers.

## Key facts and formulas

| Fact or formula | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Denominator with only primes 2 and 5 means terminating | After simplifying | Deciding terminating or recurring. |
| a/b ± c/d: use the LCM of b and d | Common denominator | Adding and subtracting. |
| a/b × c/d = ac/bd | Multiply tops, multiply bottoms | Multiplying fractions. |
| a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c | Flip the second fraction | Dividing fractions. |
| Cancel before multiplying | Divide a numerator and a denominator by a common factor | Keeping numbers small. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: terminating or recurring?

**Example.** 7/40: 40 = 2³ × 5, so it terminates: 0.175.

**Example.** 5/12: 12 = 2² × 3; the 3 means it recurs: 0.41666...

**Example — simplify first.** 9/15 = 3/5, so it terminates: 0.6. And 14/35 = 2/5 = 0.4.

### Situation: adding mixed numbers

**Example.** 2 2/3 + 1 3/4. Whole numbers: 2 + 1 = 3. Fractions: 8/12 + 9/12 = 17/12 = 1 5/12. Total: 4 5/12.

### Situation: subtracting mixed numbers

**Example.** 5 1/4 − 2 5/6 = 21/4 − 17/6 = 63/12 − 34/12 = 29/12 = 2 5/12.

**Example — improper fractions.** 11/4 − 5/3 = 33/12 − 20/12 = 13/12 = 1 1/12.

### Situation: multiplying fractions

**Example — mixed numbers.** 2 1/2 × 1 3/5 = 5/2 × 8/5 = 40/10 = 4. (Cancelling first: the 5s cancel and 8/2 = 4.)

**Example — cancel first.** 8/15 × 9/16: cancel 8 with 16 (to 1 and 2) and 9 with 15 (to 3 and 5): 1/5 × 3/2 = 3/10.

**Example — fraction of an amount.** 3/8 of 2 2/3 kg = 3/8 × 8/3 = 1 kg.

### Situation: dividing fractions

**Example.** 3 3/4 ÷ 1 1/4 = 15/4 ÷ 5/4 = 15/4 × 4/5 = 3.

**Example — how many fit.** How many 2/3 m pieces can be cut from 5 1/3 m? 16/3 ÷ 2/3 = 16/3 × 3/2 = 8 pieces.

### Situation: simplifying a calculation

**Example — use a fraction.** 0.75 × 36 = 3/4 × 36 = 27.

**Example — regroup.** 2.5 × 3.7 × 4 = (2.5 × 4) × 3.7 = 10 × 3.7 = 37.

**Example — distributive law.** 5/8 × 17 + 5/8 × 23 = 5/8 × (17 + 23) = 5/8 × 40 = 25.

## Common mistakes

- Deciding terminating or recurring before simplifying the fraction.
- Adding denominators: 1/3 + 1/4 is 7/12, not 2/7.
- Multiplying mixed numbers by multiplying the whole parts and the fraction parts separately.
- Flipping the wrong fraction when dividing (flip the second one).
- Leaving answers unsimplified, or as improper fractions when a mixed number is asked for.
