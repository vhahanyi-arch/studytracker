# Unit 3 — Decimals, percentages and rounding

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 9, objectives 9Ni.03, 9Np.01, 9Np.02, 9Nf.05 and 9Nf.06. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Standard form — 9Ni.03

Standard form writes a number as A × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ A < 10 and n is an integer. Large numbers have positive n; small numbers (less than 1) have negative n.

- 4 500 000 = 4.5 × 10⁶;
- 0.000 32 = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴.

To multiply or divide in standard form, deal with the numbers and the powers of 10 separately, then adjust so A is between 1 and 10.

### Multiplying and dividing by powers of 10 — 9Np.01

Multiplying by 10ⁿ moves the digits n places to the left (the number gets bigger when n is positive). Multiplying by 10⁻ⁿ is dividing by 10ⁿ. Dividing by 10⁻ⁿ is multiplying by 10ⁿ.

### Upper and lower limits — 9Np.02

A rounded value stands for a range of possible values. The **lower limit** is half a unit below and the **upper limit** half a unit above, where the unit is the accuracy of the rounding. A length of 12 cm to the nearest cm could be anything from 11.5 cm up to (but not including) 12.5 cm: 11.5 ≤ l < 12.5.

### Compound percentages — 9Nf.05

When a percentage change is repeated, each change applies to the new amount, not the original. Multiply by the multiplier once for each change:

final = original × (multiplier)ⁿ.

For n years of 3% growth, multiply by 1.03ⁿ. Successive different changes multiply too: +20% then −20% is × 1.2 × 0.8 = × 0.96, a 4% decrease overall.

### Estimating, multiplying and dividing decimals — 9Nf.06

Estimate by rounding each number to 1 significant figure. To multiply, multiply as whole numbers and count the total decimal places. To divide by a decimal, multiply both numbers by the same power of 10 until the divisor is a whole number.

## Key facts and formulas

| Formula | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| A × 10ⁿ, 1 ≤ A < 10 | Standard form | Very large or very small numbers. |
| (a × 10ᵐ) × (b × 10ⁿ) = ab × 10ᵐ⁺ⁿ | Multiply numbers, add powers | Calculating in standard form. |
| Limits = value ± half the unit of rounding | Range a rounded value came from | Measurements and rounded data. |
| Final = original × (1 ± r/100)ⁿ | Compound percentage change | Interest, growth, depreciation. |
| a ÷ b = (10a) ÷ (10b) | Scaling both numbers | Dividing by a decimal. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: writing standard form

**Example.** 4 500 000 = 4.5 × 10⁶. 0.000 32 = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴. 72 × 10⁵ is not in standard form: it is 7.2 × 10⁶.

**Example — back to ordinary numbers.** 6.1 × 10³ = 6 100. 9 × 10⁻³ = 0.009.

### Situation: calculating in standard form

**Example — multiply.** (3 × 10⁴) × (6 × 10³) = 18 × 10⁷ = 1.8 × 10⁸.

**Example — divide.** (8 × 10⁶) ÷ (2 × 10⁻²) = 4 × 10⁸.

**Example — in context.** Light travels 3 × 10⁸ m each second. In 500 s it travels 1.5 × 10¹¹ m.

### Situation: powers of 10

**Example.** 3.7 × 10⁻² = 0.037. 0.52 ÷ 10⁻³ = 0.52 × 10³ = 520. 45 ÷ 10² = 0.45.

### Situation: upper and lower limits

**Example — nearest whole number.** 12 cm to the nearest cm: 11.5 ≤ l < 12.5.

**Example — one decimal place.** 3.4 kg to 1 d.p.: 3.35 ≤ m < 3.45.

**Example — nearest hundred.** A crowd of 2 400 to the nearest 100: 2 350 ≤ n < 2 450.

### Situation: compound percentages

**Example — savings.** $2 000 at 3% compound interest for 4 years: 2 000 × 1.03⁴ = $2 251.02.

**Example — depreciation.** A $12 000 car loses 15% of its value each year. After 3 years: 12 000 × 0.85³ = $7 369.50.

**Example — up then down.** A price rises 20% and then falls 20%: × 1.2 × 0.8 = × 0.96. It ends 4% lower, not back where it started.

### Situation: decimal calculations

**Example — multiply.** 0.36 × 0.25: 36 × 25 = 900, four decimal places, so 0.0900 = 0.09.

**Example — divide.** 4.68 ÷ 0.12 = 468 ÷ 12 = 39.

**Example — estimate first.** 19.8 × 0.512 ≈ 20 × 0.5 = 10. (Exact: 10.1376.)

## Common mistakes

- Writing 18 × 10⁷ as the final answer: A must be between 1 and 10.
- Getting the sign of the power wrong for small numbers: 0.000 32 is 3.2 × 10⁻⁴, not 3.2 × 10⁴.
- Using 12.4 as the upper limit of 12 to the nearest whole number: it is 12.5.
- Adding compound percentages: 3% for 4 years is not 12%.
- Thinking +20% then −20% brings a price back to where it started.
