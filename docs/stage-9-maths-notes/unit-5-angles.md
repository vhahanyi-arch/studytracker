# Unit 5 — Angles

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 9, objectives 9Gg.07, 9Gg.08, 9Gg.09, 9Gg.11 and 9Gp.01. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Interior angles of a polygon — 9Gg.07

From one vertex, a polygon with n sides can be split into n − 2 triangles. Each triangle has 180°, so the interior angles add up to (n − 2) × 180°. For a regular polygon, every interior angle is that total divided by n.

### Exterior angles of a polygon — 9Gg.08

An exterior angle is between one side and the extension of the next. Walking round any polygon you turn through one full turn, so the exterior angles always add up to 360°. For a regular polygon each exterior angle is 360° ÷ n. At each vertex, interior + exterior = 180°.

### Finding missing angles — 9Gg.09

Combine the facts and give a reason for each step:

- angles on a straight line add up to 180°; around a point, 360°;
- angles in a triangle add up to 180°; in a quadrilateral, 360°;
- vertically opposite angles are equal;
- with parallel lines: alternate and corresponding angles are equal, co-interior angles add to 180°;
- base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.

### Constructing angles and regular polygons — 9Gg.11

Using only a ruler and compasses:

- **60°:** draw a line; with the compass point on one end, draw an arc crossing the line; without changing the radius, put the point where the arc crosses the line and draw another arc crossing the first. Join the end to that crossing. (It makes an equilateral triangle.)
- **30°:** bisect a 60° angle.
- **90°:** construct a perpendicular.
- **45°:** bisect a 90° angle.
- **Regular hexagon:** draw a circle; keeping the same radius, step around the circumference marking 6 points and join them.
- **Equilateral triangle:** join every other point of the hexagon construction. **Square:** draw two perpendicular diameters of a circle and join their ends.

### Bearings and scale — 9Gp.01

Bearings are measured clockwise from north, with three figures. On a map or plan with scale 1 : n, 1 cm on the map is n cm in real life. Convert the real distance to sensible units (m or km) at the end. In a scale drawing, draw each bearing with a protractor from a north line at the starting point.

## Key facts and formulas

| Formula | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Interior angle sum = (n − 2) × 180° | Any polygon with n sides | Missing angles in polygons. |
| Exterior angle sum = 360° | Any polygon | Exterior angles; finding n. |
| Regular polygon: exterior = 360° ÷ n | Each exterior angle | Regular polygons. |
| Interior + exterior = 180° | At each vertex | Moving between interior and exterior. |
| Real distance = map distance × scale factor | Scale 1 : n | Maps and plans. |
| Back bearing = bearing ± 180° | The reverse direction | Return journeys. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: interior angle sums

**Example.** A hexagon (n = 6) has interior angles adding to 4 × 180° = 720°. A regular hexagon's interior angles are each 720° ÷ 6 = 120°.

**Example — a missing angle.** Four angles of a pentagon are 100°, 115°, 120° and 95°. The sum is (5 − 2) × 180° = 540°, so the fifth angle is 540° − 430° = 110°.

### Situation: regular polygons and exterior angles

**Example — exterior angle.** A regular octagon: exterior angle = 360° ÷ 8 = 45°, interior angle = 135°.

**Example — find the number of sides.** A regular polygon has interior angles of 156°. Exterior angle = 180° − 156° = 24°. Number of sides = 360° ÷ 24° = 15.

**Example — impossible polygon.** Could a regular polygon have exterior angles of 25°? 360 ÷ 25 = 14.4, not a whole number, so no.

### Situation: missing angles with reasons

**Example.** In triangle ABC, AB = AC and angle A = 40°. The base angles are equal (isosceles triangle), so each is (180° − 40°) ÷ 2 = 70°.

### Situation: map scales

**Example.** A map has scale 1 : 50 000. Two villages are 4 cm apart on the map. Real distance = 4 × 50 000 = 200 000 cm = 2 000 m = 2 km.

**Example — real to map.** On a plan with scale 1 : 200, a 9 m wall is 900 cm ÷ 200 = 4.5 cm long.

### Situation: bearings and scale drawing

**Example.** A ship sails 8 km from A on a bearing of 120° to B. Using 1 cm to 2 km, draw a north line at A, measure 120° clockwise, and draw 4 cm. The bearing of A from B is 120° + 180° = 300°.

## Common mistakes

- Using n × 180° instead of (n − 2) × 180° for the interior angle sum.
- Mixing interior and exterior angles when finding the number of sides.
- Applying regular-polygon formulas to irregular polygons.
- Forgetting to convert units after using a map scale.
- Measuring bearings anticlockwise, or not from north.
