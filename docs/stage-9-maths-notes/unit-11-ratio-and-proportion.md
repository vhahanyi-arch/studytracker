# Unit 11 — Ratio and proportion

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 9, objectives 9Nf.07, 9Nf.08 and 9As.07. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Direct and inverse proportion — 9Nf.07

- **Direct proportion:** when one quantity doubles, so does the other. The ratio y ÷ x stays the same: y = kx. Its graph is a straight line through the origin. Example: cost and number of identical items.
- **Inverse proportion:** when one quantity doubles, the other halves. The product x × y stays the same: y = k/x. Example: number of workers and the time a job takes.

To decide which, ask: "If I had more of this, would I need more or less of that?" Then use the unitary method or the constant k.

### Ratios in different contexts — 9Nf.08

Equivalent ratios are found by multiplying or dividing every part by the same number. Ratios appear in recipes and mixtures, map scales (1 : 25 000), sharing money, and comparing groups. A ratio can be turned into fractions of the whole: in 3 : 5 the first part is 3/8 of the total. Two ratios with a common part can be combined: if a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5, make b the same in both (12) to get a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15.

### Compound measures and their graphs — 9As.07

A compound measure combines two measures:

- speed = distance ÷ time (km/h, m/s);
- density = mass ÷ volume (g/cm³, kg/m³);
- rates such as price per kg, pay per hour, litres per minute, people per km².

On a distance–time graph the gradient is the speed; a steeper line is faster and a flat line means stopped. On a graph of total cost against amount, the gradient is the price per unit.

## Key facts and formulas

| Formula | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| y = kx (y ÷ x constant) | Direct proportion | Costs, recipes, conversions. |
| y = k/x (x × y constant) | Inverse proportion | Workers and time; speed and journey time. |
| speed = distance ÷ time | Rate of travel | Journeys; distance–time graphs. |
| density = mass ÷ volume | Mass per unit volume | Materials. |
| a : b = 2 : 3 means a is 2/5 of the total | Ratio to fraction | Sharing; fraction of a mixture. |
| km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s | Converting speeds | Mixing units. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: direct proportion

**Example.** 3 kg of apples cost $7.50. One kg costs $2.50, so 8 kg cost $20.

**Example — from a table.** x: 2, 5, 8 and y: 7, 17.5, 28. y ÷ x = 3.5 every time, so y = 3.5x: direct proportion.

### Situation: inverse proportion

**Example — workers.** 6 workers take 10 days to build a wall. The total work is 6 × 10 = 60 worker-days. 4 workers take 60 ÷ 4 = 15 days.

**Example — from a table.** x: 2, 4, 5 and y: 30, 15, 12. x × y = 60 every time, so y = 60/x: inverse proportion.

**Example — speed and time.** A journey takes 3 hours at 40 km/h. At 60 km/h it takes 40 × 3 ÷ 60 = 2 hours.

### Situation: ratios in context

**Example — map scale.** On a 1 : 25 000 map, 6 cm represents 6 × 25 000 = 150 000 cm = 1.5 km.

**Example — combining ratios.** a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5. Make b equal: 8 : 12 and 12 : 15. So a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15.

**Example — fraction of a mixture.** Concrete is cement, sand and gravel in the ratio 1 : 2 : 4. In 350 kg there is 350 × 2/7 = 100 kg of sand.

### Situation: speed

**Example.** A car travels 150 km in 2 hours 30 minutes. Time = 2.5 h, so speed = 150 ÷ 2.5 = 60 km/h.

**Example — time.** At 60 km/h, 45 km takes 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75 h = 45 minutes.

**Example — converting.** 72 km/h = 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s.

### Situation: density and other rates

**Example — density.** A metal block has mass 640 g and volume 80 cm³. Density = 640 ÷ 80 = 8 g/cm³.

**Example — population density.** A town of 45 000 people covers 18 km²: 45 000 ÷ 18 = 2 500 people per km².

### Situation: reading a distance–time graph

**Example.** A graph rises 30 km in the first 30 minutes, is flat for 15 minutes, then rises 20 km in 30 minutes. Speeds: 60 km/h, 0 (stopped), 40 km/h. The first section is the steepest, so the fastest.

## Common mistakes

- Using direct proportion when the situation is inverse (more workers take less time).
- Writing 2 h 30 min as 2.3 h: it is 2.5 h.
- Mixing units, such as km and m, in a speed calculation.
- Combining ratios without first making the shared part equal.
- Reading a distance–time graph as if the height were speed.
