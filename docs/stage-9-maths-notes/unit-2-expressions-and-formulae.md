# Unit 2 — Expressions and formulae

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 9, objectives 9Ae.01, 9Ae.02, 9Ae.03 and 9Ae.04. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Simplifying terms with powers — 9Ae.01

Multiply or divide the numbers first, then use the index laws on each letter:

- 3x² × 4x³ = 12x⁵;
- 15a⁶ ÷ 5a² = 3a⁴;
- (2y³)² = 4y⁶ (square the number and multiply the index by 2);
- x⁻² × x⁵ = x³.

Only like terms (same letters and same powers) can be added: 3x² + 2x² = 5x², but 3x² + 2x cannot be simplified.

### Expanding two brackets and algebraic fractions — 9Ae.02

To expand (a + b)(c + d), multiply each term in the first bracket by each term in the second: ac + ad + bc + bd. A grid helps. Then collect like terms.

Special results to recognise:

- (x + a)² = x² + 2ax + a² (not x² + a²);
- (x + a)(x − a) = x² − a² (the difference of two squares).

Algebraic fractions work like number fractions: cancel common factors of the whole numerator and denominator, and use a common denominator to add or subtract.

### Words to expressions — 9Ae.03

Read carefully where the square, cube or root applies:

- "the square of the sum of a and b" is (a + b)²;
- "the sum of the squares of a and b" is a² + b²;
- "the square root of n, plus 3" is √n + 3; "the square root of (n plus 3)" is √(n + 3).

### Formulae with squares and cubes — 9Ae.04

Substitute carefully: square or cube before multiplying, and put negative values in brackets. To change the subject when it is squared, isolate the square and then take the square root (giving the positive root for lengths). When it is cubed, take the cube root.

## Key facts and formulas

| Formula or method | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| (a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd | Each term times each term | Expanding two brackets. |
| (x + a)² = x² + 2ax + a² | Perfect square | Squaring a bracket. |
| (x + a)(x − a) = x² − a² | Difference of two squares | Spotting quickly, and factorising later. |
| a/b + c/d = (ad + bc)/bd | Common denominator | Adding algebraic fractions. |
| If y = x², x = √y; if y = x³, x = ∛y | Undoing powers | Changing the subject. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: simplifying products and quotients

**Example.** 3x² × 4x³ = 12x⁵. 15a⁶ ÷ 5a² = 3a⁴. (2y³)² = 4y⁶. 6p²q × 2pq³ = 12p³q⁴.

### Situation: expanding two brackets

**Example — both positive.** (x + 3)(x + 5) = x² + 5x + 3x + 15 = x² + 8x + 15.

**Example — with a negative and a coefficient.** (2x − 1)(x + 4) = 2x² + 8x − x − 4 = 2x² + 7x − 4.

**Example — a square.** (x − 6)² = (x − 6)(x − 6) = x² − 12x + 36.

**Example — difference of two squares.** (x + 7)(x − 7) = x² − 49.

### Situation: algebraic fractions

**Example — cancel.** 6x²/(3x) = 2x.

**Example — add.** x/3 + x/4 = 4x/12 + 3x/12 = 7x/12.

**Example — subtract.** 2x/5 − x/10 = 4x/10 − x/10 = 3x/10.

**Example — numerators with two terms.** (x + 2)/3 + (x − 1)/2 = (2(x + 2) + 3(x − 1))/6 = (2x + 4 + 3x − 3)/6 = (5x + 1)/6.

### Situation: words to expressions

**Example.** A square has side (x + 2) cm. Its area is (x + 2)² cm² = x² + 4x + 4 cm².

**Example.** "The cube of n, divided by 4" is n³/4.

### Situation: substituting into a formula

**Example — circle area.** A = πr² with r = 4: A = π × 16 = 50.3 (1 d.p.).

**Example — negative value.** y = 3x² − 2x with x = −2: y = 3 × 4 − 2 × (−2) = 12 + 4 = 16.

**Example — kinetic energy.** E = ½mv² with m = 6 and v = 5: E = ½ × 6 × 25 = 75.

### Situation: changing the subject

**Example — square root.** Make r the subject of A = πr²: divide by π, r² = A/π, so r = √(A/π).

**Example — cube root.** Make x the subject of V = x³: x = ∛V. If V = 343, x = 7.

**Example — several steps.** Make s the subject of v² = u² + 2as: subtract u², v² − u² = 2as, so s = (v² − u²)/(2a).

## Common mistakes

- Squaring a bracket term by term: (x + 3)² is x² + 6x + 9, not x² + 9.
- Forgetting the middle terms when expanding two brackets.
- Cancelling a single term from a sum: (x + 6)/3 is not x + 2.
- Writing 3x² = (3x)²: only x is squared in 3x².
- Forgetting brackets when substituting a negative number.
