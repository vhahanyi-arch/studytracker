# Unit 14 — Volume, surface area and symmetry

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 9, objectives 9Gg.04, 9Gg.05 and 9Gg.06. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Volume of prisms and cylinders — 9Gg.04

A prism has the same cross-section all along its length, so:

volume = area of cross-section × length.

A cylinder is a prism with a circular cross-section: V = πr²h. Volume is measured in cubic units (cm³, m³). For capacity, 1 cm³ = 1 ml and 1 000 cm³ = 1 litre.

### Surface area — 9Gg.05

Surface area is the total area of all the faces. Imagine the net and add the area of each face.

- Cube: 6s². Cuboid: 2(lw + lh + wh).
- Prism: two cross-sections plus the rectangles round the side (perimeter of the cross-section × length).
- Pyramid: the base plus each triangular face.
- Cylinder: two circles plus a curved surface. The curved surface opens out into a rectangle whose width is the circumference: 2πrh. Total = 2πr² + 2πrh. An open cylinder (no lid) has only one circle.

### Planes of symmetry — 9Gg.06

A plane of symmetry cuts a 3D shape into two halves that are mirror images of each other.

| Shape | Planes of symmetry |
|---|---|
| Cuboid with three different edge lengths | 3 |
| Square-based prism (not a cube) | 5 |
| Cube | 9 |
| Prism with an equilateral-triangle cross-section | 4 |
| Regular square-based pyramid | 4 |
| Cylinder | Infinitely many |

## Key facts and formulas

| Formula | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| V = area of cross-section × length | Volume of any prism | Prisms of every shape. |
| V = πr²h | Volume of a cylinder | Tins, pipes, tanks. |
| SA cylinder = 2πr² + 2πrh | Two ends plus curved surface | Closed cylinders. |
| Curved surface = 2πrh | The label of a tin | Open cylinders, labels. |
| 1 cm³ = 1 ml; 1 000 cm³ = 1 l | Volume to capacity | How much a container holds. |
| 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ | 100 × 100 × 100 | Converting volume units. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: volume of a prism

**Example — L-shaped prism.** The cross-section is an L made of a 6 cm by 2 cm rectangle and a 2 cm by 3 cm rectangle: area 12 + 6 = 18 cm². Length 10 cm: volume = 180 cm³.

**Example — trapezium prism.** A trough has a trapezium cross-section with parallel sides 30 cm and 50 cm and depth 20 cm, and is 200 cm long. Cross-section = ½ × (30 + 50) × 20 = 800 cm². Volume = 800 × 200 = 160 000 cm³ = 160 litres.

### Situation: volume of a cylinder

**Example.** Radius 5 cm, height 12 cm: V = π × 25 × 12 = 942.5 cm³ (1 d.p.), which holds about 942 ml.

**Example — find the height.** A cylinder of radius 4 cm holds 1 litre (1 000 cm³). h = 1 000 ÷ (π × 16) = 19.9 cm.

### Situation: surface area of a cylinder

**Example — closed.** Radius 5 cm, height 12 cm: 2π × 25 + 2π × 5 × 12 = 50π + 120π = 170π = 534.1 cm².

**Example — open at the top.** Only one end: 25π + 120π = 145π = 455.5 cm².

**Example — a label.** The label round a tin of radius 4 cm and height 11 cm has area 2π × 4 × 11 = 276.5 cm².

### Situation: surface area of a prism and pyramid

**Example — triangular prism.** Isosceles triangle cross-section with sides 5, 5 and 6 cm and height 4 cm; prism length 10 cm. Two triangles: 2 × ½ × 6 × 4 = 24 cm². Sides: (5 + 5 + 6) × 10 = 160 cm². Total = 184 cm².

**Example — square-based pyramid.** Base 8 cm by 8 cm; each triangular face has slant height 5 cm. 64 + 4 × ½ × 8 × 5 = 64 + 80 = 144 cm².

### Situation: converting volume units

**Example.** A tank holds 2.4 m³ = 2.4 × 1 000 000 = 2 400 000 cm³ = 2 400 litres.

### Situation: planes of symmetry

**Example.** A cuboid 2 cm by 3 cm by 4 cm has 3 planes of symmetry, each cutting through the middle parallel to a pair of faces. A cube has 9: 3 of those, plus 6 diagonal planes through opposite edges.

## Common mistakes

- Multiplying three lengths of a non-cuboid prism instead of using the cross-section area.
- Using the diameter in πr².
- Forgetting one or both circular ends of a cylinder in its surface area.
- Converting 1 m³ to 100 cm³ (it is 1 000 000 cm³).
- Giving volumes in cm² or surface areas in cm³.
