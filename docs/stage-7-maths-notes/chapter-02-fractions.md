# Chapter 2 — Fractions, decimals and percentages

Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics, Stage 7 foundations: equivalent fractions, fraction calculations, and moving between fractions, decimals and percentages. See [source and scope](README.md).

## Key ideas

### Equivalent fractions and simplest form

Multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number gives an equivalent fraction: 3/4 = 6/8 = 9/12. A fraction is in its simplest form when no number other than 1 divides both: 15/20 = 3/4 (divide both by 5).

### Comparing and ordering fractions

To compare fractions, give them a common denominator. For 2/3 and 3/5, use 15: 2/3 = 10/15 and 3/5 = 9/15, so 2/3 is bigger. You can also change each fraction to a decimal.

### Mixed numbers and improper fractions

A mixed number such as 2 3/4 means 2 + 3/4. As an improper fraction it is 11/4 (2 × 4 + 3 = 11 quarters). Going back, 17/5 = 3 2/5, because 17 ÷ 5 = 3 remainder 2.

### Adding and subtracting fractions

With the same denominator, add or subtract the numerators. With different denominators, first change both fractions to a common denominator (the LCM of the denominators is best): 1/4 + 2/3 = 3/12 + 8/12 = 11/12.

### Multiplying a fraction by a whole number

Multiply the numerator: 3 × 2/5 = 6/5 = 1 1/5. To find a fraction of an amount, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator: 3/8 of 40 = 40 ÷ 8 × 3 = 15.

### Fractions, decimals and percentages

Per cent means "out of 100". So 0.35 = 35/100 = 35%, and 35/100 simplifies to 7/20. To change a fraction to a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator: 3/8 = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375 = 37.5%.

### Percentages of amounts

10% is a tenth (divide by 10) and 1% is a hundredth (divide by 100). Build other percentages from these: 15% of 60 = 10% + 5% = 6 + 3 = 9. To write one amount as a percentage of another, make a fraction and change it: 12 out of 48 = 12/48 = 1/4 = 25%.

## Key facts and formulas

| Fact or method | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 = 0.5 = 50% | A half | Quick conversions. |
| 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%, 3/4 = 0.75 = 75% | Quarters | Quick conversions. |
| 1/5 = 0.2 = 20%, 1/10 = 0.1 = 10% | Fifths and tenths | Building percentages. |
| 1/3 = 0.333... | Thirds recur | Recognising recurring decimals. |
| a/b of n = n ÷ b × a | A fraction of an amount | Sharing and finding parts. |
| part/whole × 100 | One amount as a percentage of another | Test scores and comparisons. |

## Using the methods in different situations

### Situation: ordering mixed forms

**Example.** Order 0.65, 5/8, 58% and 3/5 from smallest. As decimals they are 0.65, 0.625, 0.58 and 0.6, so the order is 58%, 3/5, 5/8, 0.65.

**Example — two fractions.** Which is bigger, 4/9 or 1/2? Half of 9 is 4.5, and 4 ninths is less than 4.5 ninths, so 1/2 is bigger. As decimals: 4/9 = 0.444... and 1/2 = 0.5.

### Situation: adding and subtracting

**Example — one denominator is a multiple of the other.** 2/3 + 1/6 = 4/6 + 1/6 = 5/6.

**Example — subtracting.** 5/6 − 1/4 = 10/12 − 3/12 = 7/12.

**Example — mixed numbers.** 1 1/2 + 2 2/3 = (1 + 2) + (3/6 + 4/6) = 3 + 7/6 = 4 1/6.

### Situation: fractions of amounts

**Example.** 2/5 of 35 = 35 ÷ 5 × 2 = 14.

**Example — in context.** In a class of 28, 3/7 of the students walk to school. 28 ÷ 7 × 3 = 12 students walk.

**Example — fraction × whole number.** 4 × 3/8 = 12/8 = 1 4/8 = 1 1/2.

### Situation: percentages

**Example.** 25% of 84 = 84 ÷ 4 = 21.

**Example — building a percentage.** 35% of 160: 10% is 16, so 30% is 48; 5% is 8. 35% = 48 + 8 = 56.

**Example — a reduction.** A shirt costs 40 dollars and is reduced by 20%. 20% of 40 = 8, so it now costs 32 dollars.

**Example — as a percentage.** 18 marks out of 24 = 18/24 = 3/4 = 75%.

### Situation: converting between forms

**Example.** 7/20 = 35/100 = 0.35 = 35%.

**Example — a small decimal.** 0.08 = 8/100 = 8%, and 8/100 simplifies to 2/25.

**Example — eighths.** 1/8 = 0.125 = 12.5%.

## Common mistakes

- Adding the denominators: 1/4 + 2/3 is not 3/7.
- Writing 0.08 as 80%: 0.08 is 8 hundredths, so 8%.
- Thinking a bigger denominator means a bigger fraction: 1/8 is smaller than 1/5.
- Finding a fraction of an amount by multiplying by the denominator instead of dividing.
- Leaving an answer as an improper fraction or not simplified when a simplest form is asked for.
